# Challenge_Data — cleaned DOHA decisions

Break Through Tech AI Studio, Fall 2026 · A3 Consulting challenge project.

This folder holds the cleaned dataset our work is built on, and this file explains
what was done to it. The statistics block near the bottom is regenerated by
`python -m src.validate` — everything above it is written by hand.

---

## What this is

Each row is one published decision from the U.S. Defense Office of Hearings and
Appeals (DOHA) on an industrial security clearance: either a **hearing** (an
administrative judge's first-instance ruling) or an **appeal** (a three-judge
Appeal Board review of one). Rows carry the full decision text, the outcome, which
SEAD-4 adjudicative guidelines (A–M) were at issue, and character offsets locating
the evidence for each of those inside the text.

## Where it came from

The source is the two parquet shards in [TeneikaAskew/doha](https://github.com/TeneikaAskew/doha)
(`doha_parsed_cases/all_cases_*.parquet`), which our Challenge Advisor produced by
scraping DOHA's public archive and parsing the PDFs.

**We treat that repository as read-only.** The cleaning pipeline reads its parquet
files and writes only here. Nothing in `doha/` is modified, so every result is
reproducible from the original and any change we made can be undone.

## What was wrong with the source

All of these were measured directly, not assumed.

| Problem | Scale |
|---|---|
| **Guideline labels largely fabricated** | The `guidelines` column matched the judge's actual ruling only **6.7%** of the time. Guideline A was flagged on **20,662** cases; the true count is **3**. |
| **Nothing detectable as missing** | Zero true nulls. Missing values were the strings `''` and `'Unknown'`, so `df.isna()` reported a clean dataset that was 8% unusable. |
| **Verdict leakage** | The outcome appears in the document that a model would use to predict it — before the halfway mark in **61%** of cases. |
| **Web-scrape contamination** | **7,607 rows (22.6%)** began with a browser print header (`file:///…DOHA%20transfer…[6/24/2021 3:20:16 PM]`), sitting exactly where the parser looked for the date and title. |
| **Two different rulebooks** | **12.5%** of cases were decided under Directive 5220.6 (`E2.A2.1.2.1`) rather than SEAD-4 (`AG ¶ 19(a)`). The numbering schemes are not interchangeable. |
| **Case type mislabelled** | **2,246** rows filed as `hearing` carry an `APPEAL BOARD DECISION` title. |
| **Duplicates** | Thousands of rows are byte-identical copies of another row. |
| **Malformed metadata** | 2,772 judge values contained line breaks or sentence fragments like `'Board reverses the'`; 904 dates parsed to impossible years (214, 9201). |

### The root cause

Almost all of it traces to one design choice: **the source parser searched whole
documents for every field.** A DOHA decision has a stable structure —

```
SYNOPSIS                 states the outcome up front
STATEMENT OF THE CASE    procedural history
FINDINGS OF FACT         the evidence
POLICIES                 boilerplate reciting every guideline
CONCLUSIONS              the judge's reasoning
FORMAL FINDINGS          the ruling, guideline by guideline
DECISION                 the verdict
```

Searching all of it at once means the POLICIES boilerplate gets read as guideline
labels, and the SYNOPSIS verdict gets read as input data. Two missing regex word
boundaries did the rest: `AG ¶ 2` matched inside `AG ¶ 20`, and `Guideline C`
matched inside the phrase "Guideline **c**an".

## What we did

1. **Strip the scrape header.** Removed per row by matching its trailing timestamp
   — its length varies from 136 to 169 characters, so a fixed-width cut would have
   damaged 2,572 rows. `html_prefix_len` records how much came off.
2. **Segment every document** into sections and store their character offsets.
3. **Re-read the outcome** from the verdict section alone. Appeal outcomes are a
   composition — *"the Judge's decision **denying** Applicant a clearance is
   **AFFIRMED**"* means DENIED — so they are resolved as underlying-decision ×
   board-action rather than by phrase matching.
4. **Re-read the guideline labels** from the FORMAL FINDINGS section, where the
   judge writes them explicitly (`Paragraph 1, Guideline B: AGAINST APPLICANT`),
   capturing the FOR/AGAINST direction and the character offset of each ruling.
5. **Repair the metadata** — judge names from the signature block only, dates
   validated against a plausible window and cross-checked against the docket year.
6. **Correct the case type** from document structure rather than the stored label.
7. **Delete byte-identical duplicates**, and flag — but keep — the rows where two
   *different* documents share a case number.
8. **Build leak-free feature text** from the pre-verdict sections only.

Every original value is preserved alongside ours (`outcome_stored`,
`guidelines_stored`, `judge_raw`, `date_stored`, `case_type_stored`), so no
correction is a silent overwrite.

## Label quality is tiered — read this before reporting any metric

Not every row's labels are equally trustworthy, and **metrics must never be
reported across tiers blended together**. `label_source` says which tier a row is in.

| tier | what it means |
|---|---|
| `formal_ruling` | The judge's own written ruling. Authoritative. |
| `inherited_from_hearing` | An appeal, labelled from the hearing it reviews. Sound — the Board reviews the same alleged guidelines — but inferred, not observed. |
| `regex_fallback` | Keyword matching. **~60% precision.** Treat as candidates, never as ground truth. Recall is 100%, so the true labels are always a subset of these. |
| `none` | No label recoverable. |

One caveat on precision: the obvious way to validate the `formal_ruling` tier is to
compare it against the source's `formal_findings` column — but that column was
produced by nearly the same regex, so the comparison is circular and proves
nothing. Against an independent signal (guideline sub-headings in the ANALYSIS
section), **recall is 98.0%**. Precision is not yet established and awaits a
hand-verified sample. Don't quote a precision figure until that exists.

## How to use it

```python
import glob, pandas as pd

df = pd.concat(
    [pd.read_parquet(f) for f in sorted(glob.glob("Challenge_Data/cases_clean_*.parquet"))],
    ignore_index=True,
)

train = df[df.use_for_training & (df.label_source == "formal_ruling")]
```

- **Model on `text_features`, never on `full_text`.** We measured this. Same
  TF-IDF + logistic regression, same grouped train/test split, only the input
  column changed:

  | input | accuracy | macro F1 | what the model learned |
  |---|---:|---:|---|
  | `full_text` | 95.7% | 95.4% | `is denied`, `against applicant`, `is granted` |
  | `text_features` | **82.5%** | **81.6%** | `credibly`, `paid`, `provided no`, `claimed` |
  | majority class | 64.3% | — | — |

  The 95.7% model's strongest predictors are the verdict words themselves — it is
  reading the answer, not predicting it, and would fall to roughly chance on a case
  that hasn't been decided. The 82.5% model's predictors are substantive: whether
  the applicant was found *credible*, whether debts were *paid*, whether they
  *provided no* documentation. **82.5% against a 64.3% baseline is the honest
  number**, and it is 18 points of real signal.
- **Split by grouping on `split_group`**, so a hearing and its own appeal never land
  on opposite sides of a train/test boundary.
- **Report metrics split by `guideline_regime`.** Mixing SEAD-4 and Directive-era
  cases averages two different legal standards.
- `full_text` is byte-identical to the source, and every offset column indexes it
  directly — which is what makes extracted evidence traceable.

## What is still missing

- **4,286 cases were never downloaded** and have no text here at all. Recovering
  them means re-running the scraper against DOHA.
- **`sor_allegations` is empty for ~96% of hearings.** The allegation *structure* is
  captured in `guideline_subparagraphs`, but the allegation *text* is enumerated in
  only ~13% of decisions; most paraphrase it.
- **Appeals are labelled at a lower rate** than hearings. They contain no formal
  findings section, so only those citing a hearing we hold can inherit labels.
- **The 221 case-number collisions are flagged but undiagnosed** — rehearings after
  remand, multi-case compilation pages, or extraction errors. Both documents are
  kept until someone checks a few source PDFs.
- **About a third of rows match neither citation scheme** (`guideline_regime` =
  `unknown`), suggesting a third convention we have not mapped.
